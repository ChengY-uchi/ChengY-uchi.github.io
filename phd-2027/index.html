Statement of purpose

I began in computer vision, working on recognizing emotion from gait. By the time I started working on 3D reconstruction I was mostly interested in what I could edit afterwards. One of our objects was a microphone. I wanted to pull its cable and get a reasonable bend all the way up to the attachment. For PowerFlex I worked on how to transmit a pull across the reconstruction when some of the intervening material is flexible and some of it is quite stiff. I also ran the editor on a plain beam. There I could impose exactly the same displacement as in a material point simulation and compare the two shapes.

One restriction in those experiments is that I have to start with a reconstruction. This rules out a lot of the edits I'd like to make. I might want a camera to keep going past the last view I have of a room, and I'd like a say in what it's going to find there. In PAVE I used image inpainting to lay out a view further along the camera path. Estimating its depth gave me a place to anchor it. I could then leave the views on the way there to a video generator.

I've also worked on a case where what you want from a video is information about an event. A robot has a long demonstration to follow, and somewhere in it are a few frames which are relevant to what the robot is doing now. In Decoupled Reading I built a reader to find them. For WAIT I trained a completion head so the robot could advance through the demonstration at its own pace. The improvement held up on a real robot too. There's a catch, though, if I want to use this for composition. Somebody has already put the events together for me in the demonstration. A video generator runs into much the same problem if I ask it to put together motions from two different recordings.

In the future, I'd like to try a version of the cable experiment that I can't do with either PowerFlex or PAVE alone. I'd start with footage of a cable being pulled and use the motion to work out where it was attached. Then I'd move the attachment and ask for the footage again, this time from across the table. I can imagine getting several perfectly reasonable videos back. The loose end might swing a little more in one, say, or catch on something lying on the table. What I'd want to know is whether I could pick one of those sequences halfway through, move the camera again, and find the cable still caught on the same thing. That would be a much more interesting sort of 4D reconstruction for me to work with.
